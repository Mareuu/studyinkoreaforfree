If you want to study in South Korea for free without the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), your primary key to success is securing a **Research Assistantship (RA)** directly through a laboratory director (Professor/PI).

In Korea, STEM, AI, and engineering professors manage large research budgets from corporate sponsors and government grants (like BK21). They use these funds to cover full tuition and monthly stipends for international graduate students.

However, Korean professors receive dozens of generic spam emails every week. Here is how to stand out, get your email read, and secure a funded spot in their lab.

---

## 1. When to Send Your Email

Timing is critical when applying for Korean research laboratories:

* **Spring Intake (March Start):** Send emails between **June and August** of the preceding year.
* **Fall Intake (September Start):** Send emails between **December and February**.

> **Pro Tip:** Send your email between **8:30 AM and 9:00 AM KST (Korean Standard Time)** on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. This ensures your email lands right at the top of the professor's inbox as they sit down at their desk.

---

## 2. The 3 Rules of High-Converting Cold Emails

### Rule 1: Keep It Under 200 Words
Professors do not have time to read a 5-page biography. State who you are, what you bring to the table, and why their lab specifically aligns with your goals in 3 brief paragraphs.

### Rule 2: Cite a Specific Recent Paper
Generic complimenting (*"I am deeply inspired by your world-class research"*) goes straight into the trash folder. Mention **one specific paper** published by their lab in the last 1–2 years and briefly state how it connects to your past projects or thesis.

### Rule 3: Attach a Clean 1-Page CV & Transcripts
Never send zip files or multiple image attachments. Attach **a single PDF CV** (and optionally your transcript) directly to the email, or provide a clean link to your Google Drive / GitHub profile.

---

## 3. Proven Cold-Email Template

Here is a tested email structure tailored specifically for reaching out to professors at research institutes like **GIST, KAIST, UNIST, UST, or SKY universities**:

```text
SUBJECT: Prospective [Master's / Ph.D.] Student Inquiry – [Your Name] – [Your Specific Subfield]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I hope this email finds you well. 

My name is [Your Name], and I recently graduated with a Bachelor's degree in [Your Major] from [Your University] (GPA: [Your GPA/Scale, e.g., 3.8/4.0]). I am writing to express my strong interest in joining your lab as a funded [Master's / Ph.D.] student for the [Spring/Fall 202X] semester.

I have been following your lab's work at [University Name], particularly your recent paper on "[Title of a Recent Paper published by the lab]." I was especially intrigued by your approach to [mention one specific technique or finding from the paper]. During my undergraduate studies, I worked on [briefly mention a relevant project, paper, thesis, or technical skill], where I utilized [tools/languages/frameworks like Python, PyTorch, C++, etc.]. 

Given my background in [Your Subfield], I believe I can actively contribute to your ongoing research projects while pursuing my degree. 

Could you let me know if you are expecting any Research Assistant (RA) openings or lab funding for the upcoming [Spring/Fall] intake? I have attached my CV and academic transcript for your review. 

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,

[Your Full Name]
[Link to LinkedIn / GitHub / Portfolio]
[Your Phone Number with Country Code]
