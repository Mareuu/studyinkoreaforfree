If you've sent cold emails to Korean professors, you already know the most common response is no response at all. That's normal — it doesn't mean your email was bad, and it definitely doesn't mean you should stop after one try.

---

## 1. Why Professors Don't Reply

* Your email got buried under dozens of others sent that same week.
* They currently have **no open funding slots**, and don't feel obligated to say so.
* They're traveling, at a conference, or between semesters.
* They read it, meant to reply, and forgot — this happens far more than applicants assume.

None of these mean you did something wrong. Treat silence as "not yet," not as "no."

---

## 2. The Follow-Up Timeline

* **Day 0:** Send your initial email.
* **Day 10–14:** If no reply, send one polite follow-up (template below).
* **Day 24–28:** If still nothing, send a final short follow-up, or simply move on.
* **After that:** Stop emailing that professor for this intake. Repeated messages past this point tend to hurt rather than help.

---

## 3. Follow-Up Email Template

```text
SUBJECT: Re: Prospective [Master's / Ph.D.] Student Inquiry – [Your Name]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I hope you're doing well. I wanted to briefly follow up on my email from [date] regarding potential Research Assistant / lab funding opportunities for the [Spring/Fall 202X] intake.

I understand you're likely very busy, so no worries if this isn't something you're able to consider right now. If it's helpful, I'm happy to send any additional materials, such as my transcript or a writing sample.

Thank you again for your time.

Best regards,
[Your Full Name]
```

Keep it short. The point of a follow-up is a gentle nudge, not a second full pitch.

---

## 4. Reading Between the Lines

Not every reply is a clear yes or no. Learn to recognize the in-between answers:

| What they say | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "We don't have funding available right now." | Soft rejection for this intake — ask if you can reach out again next intake. |
| "I'll discuss with my current students and get back to you." | Genuine maybe — follow up once after 2 weeks if silent. |
| "Please apply through the university's general admission first." | Not a personal rejection — they want you admitted before committing funding. |
| No reply after two follow-ups | Treat as a no and move on. |

---

## 5. Email Many Labs, Not One at a Time

The single biggest mistake applicants make is emailing one professor, waiting weeks for an answer, and only then emailing the next. Reach out to **15–30 professors in your subfield in parallel batches** instead. Since most won't reply, and only some of those who do will have funding, volume is what actually gets you a funded seat — not waiting on one perfect match.

Rejection (or silence) from most professors is the expected outcome, not a sign you should stop. Keep the batch moving.
